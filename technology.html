ta-C Diamond Coating Technology

Understanding how tetrahedral amorphous carbon (ta-C) transforms speaker diaphragm performance at the molecular level

What is ta-C?

ta-C (Tetrahedral Amorphous Carbon) is the purest form of Diamond-Like Carbon (DLC). It consists of a high proportion (>85%) of SP³-hybridized carbon atoms bonded in a tetrahedral structure similar to diamond, but without long-range crystalline order — hence "amorphous."

Unlike conventional hydrogenated DLC (a-C:H), ta-C is hydrogen-free, resulting in significantly higher SP³ content and superior mechanical properties. Its hardness and rigidity sit between standard DLC and natural diamond, making it an ideal material for high-performance speaker diaphragms.

FCVA Deposition Process

Filtered Cathodic Vacuum Arc (FCVA) is the core technology for producing high-quality ta-C thin films. In a vacuum environment, a high-current arc discharge ionizes a high-purity graphite target, generating energetic carbon plasma.

High-Purity Graphite Target

Premium graphite as carbon source material

FCVA Arc Deposition

Filtered Cathodic Vacuum Arc ionizes graphite into carbon plasma

Carbon Ion Filtration

Magnetic filter deflects pure ions, blocks macro-particles

ta-C Thin Film Deposition

Pure carbon ions form ta-C layer with >85% sp³ content

Carbon ions are deflected through a magnetic filtering system, which separates out un-ionized graphite particles and macro-particles. Only pure carbon ions pass through and deposit onto the diaphragm substrate, forming a dense ta-C thin film.

Tianbo's low-energy FCVA process operates at low temperatures (<100°C), making it compatible with polymer substrates such as PET, PEEK, and PEI, while significantly reducing manufacturing complexity and cost.

Acoustic Benefits of ta-C Coated Diaphragms

📉

Reduced Distortion

The increased rigidity shifts the breakup frequency upward, significantly reducing harmonic distortion especially in the high-frequency range. THD can be as low as 0.03%.

⚡

Improved Transient Response

High stiffness and optimized damping enable the diaphragm to respond to audio signals faster and more accurately, precisely reproducing percussion and vocal details.

📈

Extended High-Frequency Response

With sound velocity reaching 18.3 km/s (far exceeding aluminum's 5.2 km/s and beryllium's 12.3 km/s), HF extension surpasses conventional materials.

🔊

Higher Acoustic Efficiency

Enhanced mechanical properties enable more efficient conversion of electrical energy to sound, producing higher SPL and better dynamic range.
